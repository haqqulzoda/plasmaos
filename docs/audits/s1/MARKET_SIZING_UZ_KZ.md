## Method

- Source: World Bank procurement-notice API, notice type "Contract Award", project country Uzbekistan or Kazakhstan, procurement group "Consultant Services", notice date inside the window. Each notice is stored verbatim (raw text, retrieval time, content hash) and parsed deterministically: the Awarded, Evaluated and Rejected sections list every firm whose proposal was opened; firms listed together before one score/price block are one joint-venture bid.
- A bid is one proposal on one assignment. A firm is a normalized name (upper-case, punctuation and legal forms such as LLC/LTD/JSC removed) plus the firm's published country; a firm in a joint venture is counted as a bidder on that bid.
- Local = the firm's published country is the project country; foreign = any other country.
- Awarded value = the signed contract price, or the awarded bid's final evaluated price when no signed price is published; counted once per joint venture; amounts are summed per currency and not converted.

## Caveats

- Coverage is World Bank contract-award notices only. ADB and EBRD consulting awards are not in the ledger (ADB and EBRD publish winners and contract values but not the other firms that bid; see docs/audits/s1/ADB_EBRD_REJECTED_BIDDER_DISCLOSURE.md): these counts are a floor for the MDB market, not its size.
- Only assignments whose award notice is published are counted; shortlisting-only stages (expressions of interest that never reached proposals), cancelled selections and awards not yet published are missing.
- Direct Selection awards have one firm and no competition; they are included (see methods).
- Kazakhstan's World Bank consulting volume is small, so its figures rest on few notices.
- Name normalization can split one firm written two ways, or (rarely) merge two firms with the same normalized name and country.
- Sector figures overlap: a project tagged with several sectors counts in each.
- Values are as published, in the currency of the notice; a few notices publish multi-currency prices, which are kept as text and not summed. Source entry errors are kept as published (for example a BDT amount on an Uzbekistan notice).
